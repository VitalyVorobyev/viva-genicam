# Unsent clarification brief for A3 or counsel

Draft only, 2026-10-25. Nothing has been sent and no terms have been accepted.

viva-genicam is an independent open-source Rust camera library, CLI, Python
package, GenTL producer, and desktop viewer. Its code is MIT-licensed; the goal
is to allow both open-source distribution and downstream commercial use. It
implements transport interoperability without bundling A3 specification PDFs or
claiming A3 registration, certification, affiliation, or endorsement. Development
uses public documentation, comparative implementations, vendor XML and real
camera observations. Provenance requires review; we do not claim a historical
clean-room process. The maintainer reports no known A3 license or accepted
specification-download agreement.

The published GigE Vision and USB3 Vision licensing pages include software for
distribution, not only use of names and logos. We seek clarification of:

1. The legal instruments and intellectual-property rights relied on for an
   independently authored interoperable library that neither uses certification
   logos nor accepts a specification agreement. Please identify any applicable
   patent declarations or published license terms separately from trademarks.
2. Whether public MIT source distribution and free binaries require a license,
   and whether scope differs for libraries, CLI applications, Python wheels,
   GenTL producers, desktop viewers, and in-process fake devices used for tests.
3. Whether a project license can coexist with unrestricted MIT redistribution,
   forks, package-manager mirrors, and downstream commercial products, and which
   downstream parties would need their own registration or payments.
4. Which nominative compatibility descriptions are permitted without claiming
   registered compliance, and the required trademark attribution wording.
5. The evaluation/download terms and their applicability to independently
   obtained information and previously developed code. Please supply the exact
   agreement/version rather than a general registration-page description.
6. Available open-source-specific arrangements and compliance testing, without
   assuming that conformance tests grant intellectual-property rights.

For counsel: assess the above separately under German/EU and intended
distribution jurisdictions; distinguish copyright expression, necessary
interface facts, patent rights, contract formation, and trademark use. Review
GenTL ABI and SFNC/PFNC provenance alongside A3 transport questions. The desired
outcome is an evidenced distribution policy, not a blanket assertion that MIT
eliminates third-party obligations.
